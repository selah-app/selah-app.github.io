Privacy Policy for Selah

Last updated: August 8, 2026

Selah is a devotional practice app — a session timer, Bible reading schedules, and, if you want them, connections with a few people you practice alongside. This policy sets out what the app collects, why, and what you can do about it. We have tried to keep it in plain language.

What we collect

Your account. When you sign up — through Apple, Google, or an email address — we store that email address, the username you choose, and a display name if you set one. This is what lets the people you connect with recognize you.

A little about you. During setup we ask for your gender, and in some cases your birth year. These sit in a private table that no other user can read; we use them only to decide who is allowed to connect with you.

Your phone number, and finding contacts. If you ask the app to find people you already know, it reads your contact list on the device and pulls out the phone numbers. Only those numbers travel to our server, and only to be checked against other Selah users who have registered the same number — no names, no other details, and the numbers are not written down anywhere. Your contact list never leaves your phone, and giving us a phone number of your own is entirely optional.

Your practice. We keep a record of the sessions you complete — the date, the length, and how long you actually sat — along with your progress through any Bible reading you have set yourself. Keeping this on our servers rather than only on the handset is what allows your history to survive a new phone. Streaks, daily counts, and weekly averages are worked out from these records, and the people you have connected with see only what your own visibility settings let them see. You set those per person.

Reading schedules. Schedules you build are stored so they follow your account, and if you share one with someone, so that they can read it.

Notifications. Turn notifications on and we store the push token that Apple or Google issues for your device, which is what lets us send you reminders and let you know when a contact request or a companion’s activity needs your attention. Turning notifications off, in the app or in your device settings, stops this.

Roughly where you are. The first time you open the app while signed in, our server works out an approximate city, region, and country from your connection’s IP address, and keeps only that — a city, not a location. The IP address itself is used for the lookup and then discarded; it is never stored or written to a log. This happens once per account rather than continuously, and the app never asks for or uses your device’s GPS.

Anything you send us. Feedback you write from Settings reaches the developer’s inbox as an email, along with the category you picked, any screenshots you attached, and your account details, so that we can actually reply to you.

The camera. The camera is used for one thing only: scanning a contact’s QR code, if that is how you choose to add them. Nothing it sees is saved or sent anywhere.

What stays on your phone. Your session content, your preferences, a local copy of your history, and any Bible text you have read all live on the device, the last of these so that reading still works offline.

What we don’t do

There are no analytics SDKs in Selah, no advertising, and no third-party trackers. We don’t follow you around other apps or websites, and we have never sold or shared your data with advertisers or data brokers — nor will we.

Why we hold any of it

Every piece of data above earns its place by making a feature work: signing you in, keeping your history with you when you change phones, matching you with people you have chosen to connect with, showing those people what you have allowed them to see, sending the notifications you asked for, and answering your feedback. The one exception is the city we derive from your IP address, which exists so we can see, in aggregate, where in the world the app is being used. Nothing is used for any other purpose.

Who else is involved

  • Supabase runs our backend — sign-in, the database, and the server functions behind it. Data is encrypted both in transit and at rest.
  • Apple and Google handle sign-in if you choose it. Your credentials go to them, never to us; we receive only the name and email you agree to share.
  • Apple’s Push Notification service, Firebase Cloud Messaging, and Expo carry notifications to your device.
  • Resend sends the email we send — your feedback, and contact-request notifications if you have asked for those by email.
  • ipwho.is turns an IP address into a city for the lookup described above. The address goes to them for that one request and is kept by neither of us.

What you control

  • Change what your contacts can see, person by person, in Settings.
  • Remove a contact connection, or stop sharing a reading schedule, whenever you like.
  • Turn notifications off, in the app or in your device settings.
  • Refuse or withdraw the contacts and camera permissions — the app still works without them.
  • Delete your account outright from Settings → Account → Delete. This is immediate and cannot be undone: your profile, your practice history, your reading schedules, your connections, and everything shared from them are removed from our servers.

How long we keep it

Your account data stays until you delete your account, and then it goes. What is stored on your phone stays there until you delete the app or clear its data. Any feedback you have emailed us remains in the developer’s inbox, as email does, so that the conversation can be picked up again.

Children

Selah is not aimed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. If you think a child under 13 has given us information, write to the address below and we will remove it.

Changes to this policy

We will update this policy as the app changes. When something material changes, the date at the top changes with it.
