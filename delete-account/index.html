Delete your Selah account

Selah, by Nathan McGregor. Your account is yours to close, and closing it takes your data with it. There are two ways to do it — one inside the app, and one for anyone who no longer has the app installed.

From inside the app

This is immediate and needs nobody’s help:

  1. Open Selah and sign in.
  2. Go to Settings → Account.
  3. Tap Delete account and confirm.

Your account and everything listed below are removed from our servers there and then. It cannot be undone, so note anything you want to keep first.

If you no longer have the app

Email n.grantmcgregor@gmail.com from the address you signed up with, with the subject “Delete my Selah account”. If you signed in with Apple or Google, say which. We will confirm the request, delete the account within 30 days, and write back to tell you it is done.

We ask you to write from the signed-up address because it is the only thing that shows the account is yours. Where that isn’t possible — a Hide My Email address that has since lapsed, say — tell us your username and we will find another way to check.

What deletion removes

Everything held against your account, permanently:

  • your profile — username, name, email, and any phone number, gender or birth year you gave;
  • your practice history: every session you have completed, and the goals and streaks derived from it;
  • your Bible reading log and every reading schedule you have made;
  • your contacts and companion connections, and anything shared through them — which also removes you from the people you were connected to;
  • any reading schedules you had shared with others, and any shared with you;
  • the notification tokens that let us reach your devices.

What isn’t covered by it

  • Data on your phone. Selah keeps a copy of your own sessions and readings on the device so it works offline. That copy is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its storage — deleting the account does not reach onto your phone.
  • Feedback you have emailed us. Anything you sent to the address above stays in the developer’s inbox, as email does, so the conversation can be picked up again. Ask and we will delete that too.
  • Your Apple or Google account. If you signed in with one, it belongs to them rather than to us; deleting your Selah account only ends Selah’s use of it. You can also revoke Selah’s access in your Apple or Google account settings.

We keep no backup copy of a deleted account, and no retention period beyond the 30 days an emailed request may take to process.
